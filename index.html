Learning Target 1

Classifying Differential Equations & Verifying Solutions

Target
  • Identify a differential equation as linear/non-linear and determine its order.
  • Determine whether a family of functions or a function satisfies an ODE.
  • Find the function from a family that satisfies a corresponding IVP.
  1. Classify State the order of each differential equation and decide whether it is linear or nonlinear.
    • (a) \( x^3y''' - 2xy' + y = e^x \)
      Show answer

      Third order; linear

    • (b) \( y'' + yy' = \sin x \)
      Show answer

      Second order; nonlinear because of the product \( yy' \)

    • (c) \( t^2\dfrac{d^4u}{dt^4} + u = \cos t \)
      Show answer

      Fourth order; linear

    • (d) \( \left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^3 + 4y = x \)
      Show answer

      First order; nonlinear because of \( (dy/dx)^3 \)

    • (e) \( \dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} + \cos y = 0 \)
      Show answer

      Second order; nonlinear because of \( \cos y \)

  2. Verify Verify that the indicated function is an explicit solution of the given differential equation.
    • (a) \( 3y' + y = 0;\ y = e^{-x/3} \)
      Show answer

      \( y' = -\tfrac13 e^{-x/3} \), so \( 3y' + y = -e^{-x/3} + e^{-x/3} = 0 \).

    • (b) \( y'' - 4y' + 13y = 0;\ y = e^{2x}\sin 3x \)
      Show answer

      \( y' = e^{2x}(2\sin 3x + 3\cos 3x) \) and \( y'' = e^{2x}(-5\sin 3x + 12\cos 3x) \), so \( y'' - 4y' + 13y = 0 \).

  3. Interval Verify that the indicated function \( y = \phi(x) \) is a solution of the given differential equation. Give the domain of \(\phi\) as a function, then give an interval \(I\) of definition of \(\phi\) as a solution.
    • (a) \( y' = 2xy^2;\ y = \dfrac{1}{9 - x^2} \)
      Show answer

      Domain: \( \{x \mid x \ne \pm 3\} \). Interval of definition: \( (-3, 3) \); other intervals are \( (-\infty, -3) \) and \( (3, \infty) \).

    • (b) \( y' = 1 + y^2;\ y = \tan x \)
      Show answer

      Domain: all \(x\) except \( x = \tfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi \). Interval of definition: \( \left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) \) (or any one interval between consecutive asymptotes).

  4. Implicit Verify that \( \ln\!\left(\dfrac{X-2}{X-1}\right) = t \) is an implicit solution of \( \dfrac{dX}{dt} = (X-2)(X-1) \). Then find an explicit solution and an interval of definition.
    Show answer

    \( X = \dfrac{e^t - 2}{e^t - 1} \), defined on \( (-\infty, 0) \) or on \( (0, \infty) \)

  5. Verify family Verify that the indicated family of functions is a solution of the given differential equation.
    • (a) \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = 2P(1 - P);\ P = \dfrac{c_1e^{2t}}{1 + c_1e^{2t}} \)
      Show answer

      \( \dfrac{dP}{dt} = \dfrac{2c_1e^{2t}}{(1 + c_1e^{2t})^2} = 2\cdot\dfrac{c_1e^{2t}}{1 + c_1e^{2t}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1 + c_1e^{2t}} = 2P(1 - P) \)

    • (b) \( y'' - 6y' + 9y = 0;\ y = c_1e^{3x} + c_2xe^{3x} \)
      Show answer

      \( y' = (3c_1 + c_2)e^{3x} + 3c_2xe^{3x} \), \( y'' = (9c_1 + 6c_2)e^{3x} + 9c_2xe^{3x} \), so \( y'' - 6y' + 9y = 0 \).

  6. IVP \( y = \dfrac{1}{1 + c_1e^{-x}} \) is a one-parameter family of solutions of \( y' = y - y^2 \). Find the solution of the IVP with \( y(0) = -\tfrac12 \).
    Show answer

    \( y = \dfrac{1}{1 - 3e^{-x}} \)

  7. IVP \( y = 1/(x^2 + c) \) is a one-parameter family of solutions of \( y' + 2xy^2 = 0 \). Find the solution of the IVP with the given initial condition and the largest interval \(I\) over which it is defined.
    • (a) \( y(3) = \tfrac15 \)
      Show answer

      \( y = \dfrac{1}{x^2 - 4} \), \( I = (2, \infty) \)

    • (b) \( y(1) = \tfrac12 \)
      Show answer

      \( y = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + 1} \), \( I = (-\infty, \infty) \)

  8. IVP \( x = c_1\cos 2t + c_2\sin 2t \) is a two-parameter family of solutions of \( x'' + 4x = 0 \). Find the solution of the IVP with the given initial conditions.
    • (a) \( x(0) = 3,\ x'(0) = -4 \)
      Show answer

      \( x = 3\cos 2t - 2\sin 2t \)

    • (b) \( x(\pi/4) = 1,\ x'(\pi/4) = 6 \)
      Show answer

      \( x = -3\cos 2t + \sin 2t \)

  9. IVP \( y = c_1e^{2x} + c_2e^{-2x} \) is a two-parameter family of solutions of \( y'' - 4y = 0 \). Find the solution of the IVP with the given initial conditions.
    • (a) \( y(0) = 1,\ y'(0) = 6 \)
      Show answer

      \( y = 2e^{2x} - e^{-2x} \)

    • (b) \( y(1) = 0,\ y'(1) = 4 \)
      Show answer

      \( y = e^{2x-2} - e^{2-2x} \)

Learning Target 2

First-Order Existence & Uniqueness Theorem

Target
  • Apply the E&U Theorem to describe all ordered pairs over which the first-order IVP \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y),\ y(x_0) = y_0 \) is guaranteed to have a solution, and over what ordered pairs that solution is unique.
  1. Region Determine a region of the \(xy\)-plane for which the given differential equation would have a unique solution whose graph passes through a point \((x_0, y_0)\) in the region.
    • (a) \( \dfrac{dy}{dx} = y^{4/5} \)
      Show answer

      A unique solution in any region where \( y \ne 0 \) (\( y \gt 0 \) or \( y \lt 0 \))

    • (b) \( (x - 1)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y \)
      Show answer

      A unique solution in any region where \( x \ne 1 \) (\( x \gt 1 \) or \( x \lt 1 \))

    • (c) \( (9 - y^2)y' = x^3 \)
      Show answer

      A unique solution in any region where \( y \lt -3 \), \( -3 \lt y \lt 3 \), or \( y \gt 3 \)

    • (d) \( (x^2 + 4y^2)y' = x^2 \)
      Show answer

      A unique solution in any region not containing \( (0, 0) \)

  2. Uniqueness Determine whether the existence and uniqueness theorem guarantees that \( y' = \sqrt{y^2 - 16} \) has a unique solution through the given point.
    • (a) \( (2, 5) \)
      Show answer

      Yes. \( (2, 5) \) is in the region \( y \gt 4 \).

    • (b) \( (0, 4) \)
      Show answer

      No guarantee. \( y = 4 \) is on the boundary of the regions \( y \lt -4 \) and \( y \gt 4 \).

    • (c) \( (-1, -6) \)
      Show answer

      Yes. \( (-1, -6) \) is in the region \( y \lt -4 \).

Theory of Higher-Order Equations

Target

  • Determine whether functions are linearly independent.
  1. Interval Find the largest interval containing \( x = 0 \) on which the IVP \( (x + 3)y'' + 2y = x,\ y(0) = 1,\ y'(0) = 0 \) is guaranteed to have a unique solution.
    Show answer

    \( -3 \lt x \lt \infty \)

  2. Independence Determine whether the given set of functions is linearly independent on \( (-\infty, \infty) \).
    • (a) \( f_1 = x,\ f_2 = x^2,\ f_3 = 2x + 5x^2 \)
      Show answer

      Linearly dependent, since \( (2)x + (5)x^2 + (-1)(2x + 5x^2) = 0 \)

    • (b) \( f_1 = 3,\ f_2 = \sin^2 x,\ f_3 = \cos^2 x \)
      Show answer

      Linearly dependent, since \( (1)3 + (-3)\sin^2 x + (-3)\cos^2 x = 0 \)

    • (c) \( f_1 = x + 1,\ f_2 = x - 2,\ f_3 = x \)
      Show answer

      Linearly dependent, since \( (2)(x + 1) + (1)(x - 2) + (-3)x = 0 \)

    • (d) \( f_1 = 1,\ f_2 = x - 1,\ f_3 = x^2 + x \)
      Show answer

      Linearly independent: \( W(f_1, f_2, f_3) = 2 \ne 0 \)

  3. Fundamental set Verify that the given functions form a fundamental set of solutions of the differential equation on the indicated interval. Form the general solution.
    • (a) \( y'' - y' - 6y = 0;\ e^{3x},\ e^{-2x},\ (-\infty, \infty) \)
      Show answer

      \( W = -5e^{x} \ne 0 \); general solution \( y = c_1e^{3x} + c_2e^{-2x} \)

    • (b) \( x^2y'' - 4xy' + 6y = 0;\ x^2,\ x^3,\ (0, \infty) \)
      Show answer

      \( W = x^4 \ne 0 \) on \( (0, \infty) \); general solution \( y = c_1x^2 + c_2x^3 \)

    • (c) \( y'' + 4y' + 13y = 0;\ e^{-2x}\cos 3x,\ e^{-2x}\sin 3x,\ (-\infty, \infty) \)
      Show answer

      \( W = 3e^{-4x} \ne 0 \); general solution \( y = c_1e^{-2x}\cos 3x + c_2e^{-2x}\sin 3x \)
